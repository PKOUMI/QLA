EveryPupil — summary for the data protection lead

An internal tool for question-level analysis and pupil feedback, proposed for a pilot at [SCHOOL NAME].

Prepared by [YOUR NAME], [DEPARTMENT] Date [DATE] Status Pilot, not yet in use

1. What it does

A teacher enters exam marks question by question. The tool calculates totals and grades against boundaries the teacher sets, shows which topics the class found hardest, and emails each pupil a personalised summary of their own performance with suggested revision. It replaces a spreadsheet plus thirty hand-written comments.

What it does not hold: no addresses, no dates of birth, no photographs, no SEN or pupil premium flags, no attendance or behaviour records, no free-text comments about individual children, and no special category data of any kind.

Data is not transferred outside the UK or EU in normal operation. All connections use HTTPS. There are no advertising or analytics trackers in the application, and pupil data is never sold, shared, or used to train AI models.

4. Who can see it

  • Only staff explicitly added to the pilot can sign in. There is no self-registration — an account must be created for you.
  • Access is enforced by the database itself, not only by the application. A fault in the application code returns no data rather than the wrong data.
  • Sign-in is by a one-time link sent to a school email address. No passwords are stored.
  • Pupils do not have accounts and cannot sign in. They receive an email and nothing more.
  • Parents are contacted only where a teacher has ticked that pupil individually. Excluding one child from parental contact does not exclude them from feedback — this was a deliberate safeguarding requirement.

5. How long it is kept, and how it is removed

  • Pupil records and marks are kept while the pilot runs, and deleted at the end of it unless the school decides to continue.
  • Any pupil, assessment or the entire dataset can be deleted on request, immediately.
  • All data can be exported to CSV at any point.
  • Sent emails are logged as a count and timestamp for audit. The content is not retained after sending.

6. Lawful basis

The school remains the data controller throughout. The processing supports the school's public task in providing education — the same basis as reporting assessment outcomes to pupils by any other means. The tool changes how feedback reaches a pupil, not what the school is entitled to tell them about their own work.

Approval to pilot the tool with [NUMBER] members of staff and [NUMBER] pupils in [DEPARTMENT] during [TERM], and a view on whether a data protection impact assessment is required before it begins.

Stated plainly: this tool was written by a member of staff and has not been independently security tested or certified. It is proposed as a departmental pilot, not a whole-school system. If it is adopted more widely, or offered to other schools, it should be reassessed properly at that point — including a full DPIA, a written processing agreement, and independent security assurance.
